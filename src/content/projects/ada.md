summary: Can a sign be read as a sculpture first, and a name second?
intro: When designing the branding for Anthony Davis Architects (ADA) I always pictured the logo as a 3 dimensional object. I wanted to use the archetypical geometric forms of a circle, square and triangle which were perfectly suited to the ADA letters. Tony understood my thinking straight away and convincing him that he needed a 3 dimensional version of his logo on the side of his premises was an easy sell. Originally I intended making the 3 shapes from reinforced pumice concrete - using the special mix I’d formulated when building my pizza oven. This idea was abandoned after I worked out that each shape would be at least 8 kilos - too much for the cantilever to support. We switched to polystyrene with a coarse render. Anchoring rods could be concealed so the drama of the cantilever was preserved. Technically this is a design piece rather than art - but I figure that if you view the 3 forms as abstract shapes rather than letters, it classifies as art. You can see it on the side of Tony’s building at 47 Mt Eden Road, Auckland.
